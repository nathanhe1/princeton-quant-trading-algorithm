# PQT strategy: full analysis and comparison scorecard

**Strategy:** `strategies/alpha.py` (v3: v2 plus a fair-value cap and a news rebase) · **Date:**
2026-11-05 · **Data:** the 24 recorded sessions in `data/` (45 min each, 5,400 batches of 500 ms)

This document describes the strategy and its model, how it was evaluated, and every result,
including what failed. Section 1 and section 12 are written so another strategy or model can be
scored the same way and compared directly.

---

**Bottom line.** The strategy is consistently positive under every fill model tested, and the
forecast holds up out of sample. Its dollar figure is driven by how many fills it gets, and the

accuracy. The most likely live range is **$1,000–$1,700 per session** before any news alpha. Live
fill share is unknown, so this range is a judgement, not a measurement.

## 2. The competition, briefly (what is being optimised)

- **Symbols.** Four symbols trade: HPPO is an ETF whose true value is exactly 1 BNKY + 2 BRRY;
  BNKY, CRRT and BRRY are stocks.
- **Limits and fees.** Position limits are 1,000 / 2,500 / 2,500 / 600 shares. Net exposure,
  weighted HPPO 1, BNKY 0.15, BRRY 0.425 and CRRT −0.29, must stay within ±600. The taker fee is
  0.4¢/share and the maker rebate 0.2¢. Messages are capped at 20 per batch for the whole team.
- **Score.** For each fill: quantity × (true value 60 s later − price) − fees. Any news jump that
  arrives after the fill is removed from the reference. P&L and end-of-session positions do not
  count. Final = R1 + 2 × R2. So the target is *per-fill predictive edge × volume*; inventory
  matters only through limits.
- **Competition.** 60–70 teams trade live in the same market; the recorded data contains none of
  our orders and no news.

## 3. The strategy

Every batch (500 ms), for each symbol:

1. **Book without us.** Remove our own resting orders from the top of book (live only).
2. **Fair value.** fair = microprice + a linear forecast of the mid's move over the next 60 s,
   capped at about 4× the typical forecast (HPPO 20¢, BNKY 6.5¢, CRRT 8.5¢, BRRY 20¢). Microprice
   is (bid × ask size + ask × bid size) / (total size).
3. **Take.** If fair − ask > 0.4¢ fee + `take_edge` (6¢) × symbol multiplier, buy with an
   immediate-or-cancel order (IOC) at the ask; mirror image for selling. Clip: 100 / 300 / 300 /
   60 shares × `size_scale` 1.5.
4. **Make.** centre = fair − `SKEW` × position / cap. Rest a bid at centre − `make_edge` (2.5¢)
   × multiplier and an ask at centre + the same, never crossing the market. Multipliers: HPPO 1.0,
   BNKY 0.5, CRRT 0.6, BRRY 1.0. Skew at the cap: 4.5 / 1.5 / 2.25 / 4.5¢. Quote size: 50 / 150 /
   150 / 30 × 1.5. One order per side, moved with as few messages as possible; the side getting
   out of the way moves first, so we never self-trade.
5. **Risk.**
   - **Caps:** soft position caps of 400 / 1,500 / 1,500 / 400 shares; net exposure at most 580.
     Both are checked against the worst case where every open order fills.
   - **Stand-asides:** skip a symbol while it is halted, while its book is one-sided, and for 20
     batches after a halt.
   - **Startup and kill switch:** a 30-batch warm-up, and a kill switch at a $20k realised loss.
6. **News.**
   - **Pause:** on any news item, cancel all quotes for `news_pause` (10) batches. Taking stays on.
   - **Rebase (new in v3):** when the pause ends, shift the pre-news price history by the move
     since the news and zero the pause's trade flow, so the jump is not read as momentum.

Diagrams: `diagrams/alpha-batch-flow.png` (the decision flow) and `diagrams/price-ladder.png`
(worked HPPO example: where the quotes go and when a take fires).

**GUI-tunable live:** `make_edge`, `take_edge`, `size_scale`, `make_on`, `take_on`,
`news_pause`, `kill_floor`.

## 4. The fair-value model

**Target.** For each symbol separately, the mid 120 batches (60 s) ahead minus the current
microprice.

**Features (33, shared by all four symbols).**
- Microprice change over the last 10 / 30 / 60 / 120 / 240 batches, for each of the 4 symbols (20).
- Signed aggressor volume over the last 30 / 120 batches, in thousands of shares, for each symbol (8).
- Microprice − mid, for each symbol (4).
- `gapB` = (HPPO − BNKY)/2 − BRRY, i.e. BRRY's distance from its ETF-implied price (1).

**Fit.** Ridge regression with no intercept and penalty 1e-3 × n on the raw features, pooled over
sessions (`research/fit.py`). The live coefficients use all 24 sessions; every evaluation uses the
fold coefficients.

**What it learned.**
- **Momentum:** prices trail the true value.
- **Order flow:** signed flow predicts the next minute.
- **BRRY catches up to the ETF:** about 50% of `gapB` closes within 60 s.
- **CRRT moves opposite HPPO**, with a delay.
- **Microprice − mid barely persists:** its coefficients are −0.75 to −0.94, so fair sits near
  the mid with only a small tilt toward the microprice.

**Accuracy and overfitting** (`research/overfit.py`):

| model | HPPO IS / OOS | BNKY IS / OOS | CRRT IS / OOS | BRRY IS / OOS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **live: 33 features, ridge 1e-3 (raw)** | 0.143 / 0.118 | 0.104 / 0.078 | 0.178 / 0.158 | 0.309 / 0.286 |
| 33 features, scaled ridge, penalty by inner CV | 0.138 / 0.123 | 0.098 / 0.081 | 0.176 / 0.159 | 0.307 / 0.288 |
| own-symbol features only | 0.121 / 0.111 | 0.078 / 0.072 | 0.111 / 0.103 | 0.258 / 0.250 |
| own + hand-picked cross terms | 0.136 / 0.126 | 0.088 / 0.082 | 0.153 / 0.145 | 0.297 / 0.284 |

- The gap between in-sample and out-of-sample R² is 0.020–0.026. That is modest; the model is not
  badly overfit.
- Out-of-sample slope is 0.91–0.97, so predictions are about the right size.
- Cross-symbol terms carry real signal; CRRT loses a third of its R² without them.
- **Weak spots:**
  - Only 61–82% of coefficients keep their sign across folds; the small ones are noise.
  - BNKY's model fails when fitted on only 6 sessions (out-of-sample R² below zero in some blocks).
- **Horizon:** a 120-batch target beats 180 and 240 on R² for every symbol and on markout ($5,600 vs
  $4,734 vs $4,365). `research/model.py`; chart `research/charts/05_horizons.png`.

## 5. How it was evaluated (and the known biases)

- **Out of sample.** Every session is scored by coefficients fitted without it (`research/cv.py`
  folds s01–08, s09–16, s17–24).
- **Proxy.** The mid 60 s after the fill stands in for the true value. The mid 120 s after scores
  about 9% higher ($6,079 vs $5,600), so the true value may lead the mid and the proxy may
  *understate* the edge. Choosing the horizon by this proxy is biased toward 120.
- **Stressed fills** (`research/stress.py`, monkeypatching the SDK's resting-fill rule):
  - `through=N`: the trade must print N ticks through our price.
  - `share=p`: we get a fraction p of each trade, with seeded random rounding.
  - `adverse`: we are also filled on prints exactly at our price when the mid 120 batches later has
    moved past our price, i.e. a real pick-off.
- **Synthetic news** (`research/evalx.py`):
  - Each session gets 6 items, each on BNKY, BRRY or CRRT, jumping ±2× or ±4× that symbol's typical
    60 s move (HPPO 12.6¢, BNKY 4.9¢, CRRT 4.9¢, BRRY 8.2¢), propagated through the ETF.
  - The recorded book and trades reprice linearly over D batches.
  - Items reach `on_news` as live, and markouts apply the score's rule of removing later news.
  - The sizes and speeds are invented, so these runs are sensitivity tests.
- **Tuning discipline.** Strategy knobs were checked by nested CV (`research/tune_nested.py`): the
  choice never sees the scored sessions, and neither do the coefficients used while choosing.
- **Known biases, ranked:**
  1. **Volume:** the backtest gives us every fill the tape allows, with no competition.
  2. **Look-ahead:** the `adverse` rule leaks the future into our inventory (section 7).
  3. **Proxy:** the proxy is not the true value.
  4. **News:** the data contains none.
  5. **Earlier selection:** `make_edge`, `size_scale` and `take_edge` were first chosen on all 24
     sessions; nested CV shows that bias is small (section 8).

## 7. The stress test and its look-ahead

- **Original bug.** `adverse` filled at-price prints whenever the future mid had moved *at all*
  against us; most such fills were still profitable. "Adverse" alone scored **$6,752, above base**.
- **Fix.** Fill only if the mid 120 batches later is past our price, i.e. a guaranteed losing fill.
  Realistic dropped from $1,755 to $1,540.
- **Remaining issue.** Even fixed, the rule decides fills using the future price. Every forced fill
  tells the strategy where the price is going, and the inventory skew then leans its quotes the
  right way. Splitting realistic-scenario maker fills:

| | fills at exactly our price | all other fills |
|---|---|---|
| without adverse (30% + 2 ticks) | $67 (1.50¢/sh) | $1,452 (2.17¢/sh) |
| with adverse | **−$693** (−2.49¢/sh, 28k sh) | $2,000 (2.74¢/sh) |

The pick-offs cost what they should (−2.5¢/share). The other fills gain $548/session from leaked
information, so the **conservative realistic figure is about $990/session**. A real market also
leaks *some* information through who trades with you, so the truth is between $990 and $1,540. Any
strategy whose gain under `adverse` comes from inventory-driven quoting should be checked this way.

- **R² drops on test for every symbol:** about 0.03 for HPPO, BNKY and BRRY, and 0.02 for CRRT.
  BNKY is the least stable; in fold 1 its test R² is only 0.022.
- **Base markout shows no train/test gap** ($5,600 both). The fitting noise in the coefficients does
  not turn into better trading on the training sessions.
- **Realistic stress shows a small gap:** $1,628 train vs $1,540 test (−5%), and maker edge 1.35¢
  vs 1.29¢. That is the honest size of the overfit at the strategy level.
- **Session difficulty matters more than train vs test.** s01–s08 are the hardest sessions in any
  role, which is why fold 1's test is lowest and fold 2's test is highest. Compare models on the
  same sessions (paired), never across different splits.
- **Chronological test (later sessions) is not worse than train:** $5,761 vs $5,575 base, $1,561 vs
  $1,539 realistic. There is no sign that the relationships decay over time across the data.

**Conclusion:** the reported numbers are not inflated by fitting. The dominant error is the fill
model (section 7 and bias 1), not overfitting.

## 9. News

**v3 changes.**
- **Rebase:** removes the news jump from the features after the pause.
- **Fair cap:** limits |fair − micro|.
- **Effect:** no change without news. Results with synthetic news:

| repricing speed | v2 | v3 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| instant | $5,137 | $5,387 | +$249 |
| over 10 s | $5,391 | $5,455 | +$64 |
| over 30 s | $5,500 | $5,461 | −$39 |
| realistic stress + news at 10 s | $1,644 (worst $673) | $1,670 (worst $807) | +$26 |

**Variants tested:**
- **Rebase:** worth +$160 with instant repricing and +$58 at 10 s; it costs $32 at 30 s, where part
  of the post-pause move is real continuation.
- **Cap:** worth $0–20; insurance, not alpha.
- **30-batch pause:** better at 10 s, worse otherwise; kept at 10.
- **Pausing takes during the news window** (tested and reverted):
  - with instant repricing it is +$77, because those takes lose (taker edge −0.12¢ on 5.6k
    shares/session);
  - with 10 s repricing it is −$125, and −$173 under stress, because taking in the jump's
    direction while the market catches up is the credited news edge.

  The right behaviour depends on live repricing speed, which only the sandbox can show.

**Not built:** reading the headline to decide which symbols move and in which direction, then
trading it (prompt 5). This is the largest untested source of credited score.

## 11. Risks and open work, by expected impact

1. **Fill share and adverse selection** (largest). Pick-offs cost more than the maker edge, and
   BNKY's edge drops to 0.59¢ under stress. Next steps:
   - quote one-sided when |fair − micro| is large;
   - widen BNKY;
   - check maker ¢/share in R1's report, and widen `make_edge` if it is below about 1¢.
2. **News trading is not built** (prompt 5). The stress results suggest taking in the news
   direction during repricing is profitable; we need the real headline format and repricing speed
   from the sandbox.
3. **Capacity.** The strategy reaches its exposure and position caps. Ideas from prompt 3 to try:
   - conversion to free HPPO and BRRY room;
   - spending exposure where edge per unit of exposure is highest;
   - CRRT as a hedge.
4. **ETF identity is unused** for HPPO and BNKY forecasts and for arbitrage (prompts 2 and 4).
5. **Proxy vs true value.** The horizon choice is biased toward the proxy; the true value may lead.
6. **Not yet verified on the sandbox:** removing our own orders from the live book, the
   model-parameter panel, and reconnect handling (prompts 6 and 7).

## 12. How to compare another model with this one

**Run the same harness.** For a strategy that reads `self.model` (fold coefficients, out of sample):

```bash
cd research && ../.venv/bin/python scorecard.py ../strategies/OTHER.py --label other
```

For a strategy with its own built-in model, add `--coefs none`. If that model was fitted on these
24 sessions, its numbers are **in sample**: penalise it or refit per fold. The output,
`scorecards/other.md`, has the same tables as section 6. `scorecards/other_sessions.csv` holds
every session × case for paired tests.

**Compare in this order** (most to least important for the live score):

| # | metric | why | this strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | realistic stress mean **and** its look-ahead-removed value | closest to live conditions | $1,540 / about $990 |
| 2 | realistic worst session | R2 counts double; a bad session hurts | $473 |
| 3 | maker ¢/share under realistic stress | survives any volume assumption | 1.30¢ |
| 4 | harshest-stress mean and worst | robustness | $788 / −$4 |
| 5 | out-of-sample R² and in-sample−out-of-sample gap per symbol | model quality and overfitting | table in section 4 |
| 6 | news cases (instant / 10 s / 30 s) | live has news; data does not | $5,387 / $5,455 / $5,461 |
| 7 | base mean | upper bound only; overstates volume 3–6× | $5,600 |
| 8 | operational: messages max, rejects, `on_batch` time | must not break limits live | 9 / 0.1 / 47 ms |

**A difference counts only if** it holds session by session. Stress cases carry about $30 of seed
noise per configuration, so under about $60/session needs more seeds (`STRESS_SEED=11`, `12`, …)
and a paired test on the sessions CSV.

**Questions to put to the other model's write-up:**
- Are all numbers out of sample, and were the knobs chosen without seeing the scored sessions?
- Which fill model was used? A strategy judged only on the base backtest is judged on the wrong
  thing.
- Does it report per-share edge and the worst session, or only mean dollars?
- Does any stress or adverse logic use future prices, and does inventory-driven quoting benefit
  from that?
- How does it behave on news (pause, rebase, take)? Has it been tested with injected news?
- Does it respect the 20-message team budget, the ±600 exposure limit and halts?
- Does it use the ETF identity HPPO = BNKY + 2 BRRY?
